A better way to choose care.

The redesign aimed to make finding care feel more like a familiar shopping experience. Members could compare providers using information such as prices and reviews, rather than beginning with a name and little context.

For more than one million members, that promise depended on a coordinated launch across the secure and non-secure websites. The change had to reach the right people internally and preserve members’ access to the tool.

The choice: make the rollout a shared plan.

My most important decision was to build a cohesive, detailed rollout strategy in a shared Smartsheet project plan. I wanted stakeholders to know what was happening, how the launch was progressing, and when the change would take effect.

There were two risks behind that decision: an internal team discovering the launch too late, and a member being unable to access the tool because of an implementation error. Keeping the rollout visible gave the work a common point of coordination.

Connect the teams around the launch.

I was responsible for coordinating the updates to the secure and non-secure websites and preparing the go-to-market strategy across 15 lines of business. I also led sales enablement materials and change management activities as the project evolved.

The work brought together product engineers, I/S, corporate communications, and sales. My role was to keep their contributions connected to the launch and make sure the people explaining the change were prepared.

Validate the member’s path.

The work took place in 2022, with a January 1, 2023 go-live. Launch testing used member impersonation tools to check access from the member’s perspective.

The switchover completed with no implementation errors identified through that launch testing. The result was a coordinated transition that kept member access at the center of the rollout.
